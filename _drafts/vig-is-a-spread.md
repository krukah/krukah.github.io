A sportsbook posts -110 on both sides and calls the difference the vig. The
framing is that you are paying a commission. You are not. You are crossing a
spread, and the spread is wide because you cannot see the other side of it.

Convert the two prices to implied probabilities and they sum to more than
one. The excess is the overround, and it is exactly the market maker's edge
in any other asset class: the gap between the price at which they will buy
and the price at which they will sell.

The difference is disclosure. On an equity exchange you see the book, so you
can decide whether the spread is worth crossing, and you can post inside it
if it is not. At a sportsbook you cannot post at all. There is one quote, it
is theirs, and your only options are to take it or leave.

That is the whole argument for an exchange. Not that the spread should be
zero — someone has to be paid for standing in the middle — but that you
should be able to see it, and to compete with it.
