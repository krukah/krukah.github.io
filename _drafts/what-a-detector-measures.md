A common misreading of a detector diagram is that the concentric rings are
looking at the collision. They are not looking at anything. Each layer is a
device that reports where charge was deposited in it, and nothing else.

A track is not observed. It is fitted. You get a few dozen points in three
dimensions, you assume a helix because the magnetic field says you should,
and you solve for the helix that best explains the points. Curvature gives
momentum, sign of curvature gives charge, and the extrapolated origin gives
you a vertex — which is itself a fit, over several tracks that appear to have
started in the same place.

By the time anyone says the word "particle" there have been four inference
steps and every one of them has an error bar. This is not a weakness of the
method. It is the method. The discipline is in propagating those bars
honestly all the way to the claim at the end, and in being loud about the
ones you cannot propagate.
