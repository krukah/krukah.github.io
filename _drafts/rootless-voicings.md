The first thing a jazz piano teacher takes away from you is the root. It
feels like vandalism and it is the single most useful thing I learned.

A dominant seventh chord has four notes. Two of them do almost no work: the
root, which the bass is already playing, and the fifth, which is implied by
everything else and which barely moves between chords. The third and the
seventh are the whole identity. They tell you the quality and they tell you
where the chord wants to resolve.

So you play those two, plus whatever colour you want on top, and you leave
the rest to the band. The hand stops leaping. Voice leading becomes almost
automatic, because the third of one chord is usually a semitone from the
seventh of the next.

It is lossy compression with a very good model of the receiver. You are
allowed to drop the redundant bits because you know the listener will
reconstruct them.
